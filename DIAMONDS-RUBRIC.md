# The Diamonds bar

Four dimensions, scored on RISK's existing 0–10 ladder.

**Only Round 2 uses all four.** Round 1 scores one named thing on the same
ladder, against a number fixed when its piece went up. Round 0 uses no numbers
at all — it is a pass or a fail, with written feedback either way.

Scoring ladder: **[Visual Representation of 0-10 Scoring](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1jSX7X4K1KB6-2i0TXKA-zR-5A4mak1MLwxCFewSzSLs/edit)**.
Nothing here replaces it — the deck is the reference, this is how it gets
applied to an audition.

## The dimensions

1. **Technicality**
2. **Execution**
3. **Performance**
4. **Musicality & Timing**

**Your deck already scores on these four.** Read the SEULGI entry as four
sentences in order: *body control, clean lines, refined isolations* → *sharpness
and fluidity* → *confident and quietly commanding* → *rhythmic awareness and
nuance*. MOMO, NAYEON and GISELLE follow the same four. So this isn't a new
framework laid over your scoring; it's the structure your scoring already has,
named so two people can use it the same way.

## The ladder

Unchanged from the deck:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **10** | Strong idol dancer status |
| **8.75** | Can see notable ability in multiple areas |
| **7.5** | Can see notable ability in a couple areas |
| **7.1** | **RISK current benchmark** |
| **6.25** | Can see evidence of ability on a positive trajectory |
| **5** | Can see evidence of ability but not notable |
| **3.75** | Attempt is recognizable but bad |
| **2.5** | Can make out an attempt |
| **1.25** | Cannot tell intention |
| **0** | Almost nonexistent |

*notable = "oh? okay, not bad!"*

The steps are 1.25 apart, which matters later: **one anchor step is the natural
unit of improvement**, so Round 1's rule can be stated in steps rather than in
invented decimals.

### The two top anchors count areas, and that's useful

8.75 is *"notable in **multiple** areas"* and 7.5 is *"notable in a **couple**
areas"* — those describe a whole dancer, not one dimension, which looks like a
conflict with per-dimension scoring. It isn't. It means the per-dimension scores
**derive** the overall number instead of competing with it:

| Dimensions scoring ≥ 7.5 | Overall reads as |
| --- | --- |
| 3–4 | 8.75 — notable in multiple areas |
| 2 | 7.5 — notable in a couple areas |
| 0–1, but ability is visible | 6.25 — positive trajectory |

So score the four, count the notables, and check the result against the deck's
named examples. If the arithmetic says 8.75 and your gut says "that's a Gyuvin,"
trust the gut and find which dimension you over-scored.

## What "notable" looks like, per dimension

The hinge is 7.5. Descriptors below are your deck's own language, sorted.

### Technicality
- **notable** — clean isolations; commits to full extension; whole body engaged; foundational technique visible (popping, locking, levelling)
- **not notable** — stiffness when zeroed in on; dead-hand; only one section of the body doing the work; approximate shapes

### Execution
- **notable** — hits the pictures clean at tempo; soft-to-sharp texture applied as the choreography asks; holds up in the hard section
- **not notable** — reads like marking; cuts through sections; hits pictures only roughly; cleanliness drops when the tempo turns over

### Performance
- **notable** — facials present throughout, not just on their part; moves done with intention rather than plain execution; draws focus without forcing it
- **not notable** — stale presence; looks at the ground when not centered; committed only when featured

### Musicality & Timing
- **notable** — accents, holds and prolonged movements read as interpretation of the track; natural groove present; tight to rhythm
- **not notable** — reads as muscle memory rather than musicality; timing goes funky under coordination load; textures present but unused

## Round 2 — the absolute bar

**Pass = weighted score ≥ 7.5, and no single dimension below 6.**

| Dimension | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Technicality | **40%** |
| Execution | **30%** |
| Performance | **20%** |
| Musicality & Timing | **10%** |

The ordering is a judgement about what a competition unit needs most: technique
is the hardest thing to fake or coach quickly, and musicality counts least
because it's the most improvable with time in the room.

**The floor matters more under weighting, not less** — which is worth seeing
concretely. Unweighted, a weak dimension drags the average toward itself.
Weighted, a dimension at 10% barely moves the number:

```
10 / 10 / 10 / 0   ->  0.4(10) + 0.3(10) + 0.2(10) + 0.1(0) = 9.0
```

A **zero** in musicality, and the weighted score sails past any plausible bar.
The floor is the only thing standing between that and a pass, which is exactly
why it exists.

Two deliberate choices, both worth arguing with.

**Why 7.5 and not 7.1 — settled.** 7.1 is the *RISK benchmark*, the house
average. Diamonds is the competition unit, and the spec sets its bar at *"the
caliber at which we expect Diamonds to be able to execute"*, which has to sit
above the house average or it isn't a unit, it's a roster. So the bar is **7.5 —
notable ability in a couple of areas**, one real anchor above the house.

Worth knowing what that implies in practice: **passing Diamonds means being
measurably above the median RISK dancer**, not merely competent. When you write
a rejection, that's the honest framing — *you're at or near where RISK is, and
Diamonds asks for a step beyond it* — which is a much kinder thing to be told
than "not good enough".

**Why a floor of 6.** In a competition unit a dimension at 5 (*"evidence of
ability but not notable"*) is a liability nothing else offsets — it's visible on
stage next to three people who have it.

6 sits deliberately **off the ladder's anchors**, between 5 and 6.25. That's
fine: a floor isn't a description of a dancer, it's a line, and "a hair under
positive trajectory" is a reasonable place to draw one. It's slightly more
forgiving than 6.25 would be, which matters most for the dimension someone is
being stretched on — they may still be mid-climb when Round 2 arrives.

## Round 1 — improvement against a number you fix yourselves

Your model, worked through. Round 1 doesn't ask "are they Diamonds yet" — it
asks **what they did with a piece chosen to expose one specific weakness.**

```
Round 0  →  pass or fail, and the written feedback         (no numbers)
assign   →  pick the piece, name the one thing it tests,
            and score that one thing *now* — fixed here
Round 1  →  re-score that same one thing
         →  advancement turns on the change
```

**The before-number is set at assignment, not at Round 0**, and that placement
is the whole design. See "Why Round 0 isn't the baseline" below — the short
version is that a cover someone chose and a piece they were handed are not
comparable reads, so a delta between them measured nothing.

**Advance if either:**

- the target gained **at least one full anchor step (+1.25)**, or
- the target was **already ≥ 7.5** and did not regress

Against your own examples, on the deck's scale:

| | Before | After | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "gave them a 1, they get a 6" | 1.25 | 6.25 | **+4 steps** | advance |
| "started 7, became 8" | 7.5 | 8.75 | +1 step, and already at bar | advance |
| no real movement from low | 5 | 5 | 0 steps, below bar | no |
| already strong, held it | 8.75 | 8.75 | 0 steps, above bar | advance |

**The best property of this is what it refuses to do.** A pure absolute bar at
Round 1 would cut whoever started lowest, which is the same people every cycle.
A delta bar cuts whoever *didn't move* — so someone at 1.25 who fought their way
to 6.25 goes to Round 2 and is told the truth there, and someone who sat at 5
doesn't. That's a fairer filter and it's a better signal about who's worth
coaching.

**Watch all four at Round 1 even though you only score one.** The bot's Round 1
ballot takes the target and nothing else, on purpose — a second set of numbers
that decides nothing is a set of numbers that will eventually get used to decide
something. But the thing worth catching is someone who fixed their weakness by
neglecting everything else, so if you see that, it belongs in your `/diamonds
feedback` note: a conversation, not an automatic cut.

### Four safeguards, because two directors set both numbers

This is the part that makes or breaks the delta model. With no third opinion,
the same two people record the before and the after.

1. **Fix the before-number as you assign the piece, and never after.** The
   system enforces this: `/diamonds assign` takes the target and its starting
   score together and refuses to move either once set. The reason it's safe to
   set it *there* is that the video it will be compared against does not exist
   yet — so the number can only be an honest read. Leave it editable afterwards
   and it drifts toward justifying whatever you end up deciding, which is not a
   thing anyone does on purpose.
2. **Score the Round 1 submission blind to the other director, then reveal.**
   Exactly the anti-anchoring logic already in `review.ts`'s sealed ballots —
   "she said 6, so I should probably say 6 too" is the failure, and it's
   invisible from the inside. `/diamonds score` tells you only how many ballots
   are in until the last one lands.
3. **Tell them the thing *and* the number.** Currently both: the Round 1 panel
   quotes the before-number when the piece goes up, and the result quotes both
   ends. A delta you can't see the start of is a verdict rather than a
   measurement, and since you now set the before yourselves, showing it is also
   what makes it checkable. The cost is that a number invites a negotiation
   about the number, and with two directors there's no appeal process to hold
   that conversation in. **[arguable]** — it's a line in `targetLine`.
4. **Calibrate before you score anybody.** Your deck says it outright — *"the
   rankings are Alina's visualizations, but they might be different for you."*
   Both of you independently score two or three of the deck's own reference
   videos, compare, and talk about the gaps. An hour, once, before you assign
   anybody's Round 1 piece. It's the only defence you have against two private
   ladders, and the whole delta model sits on the before-number being a real
   read — which is now *your* read, typed in by hand, so this matters more than
   it did, not less.

### Why Round 0 isn't the baseline

An earlier version of this scored Round 0 on all four dimensions, made the
lowest the Round 1 target, and used its score as the before-number. It was wrong,
and worth keeping the diagnosis because the failure is invisible from inside.

**They chose the piece.** Your deck names the pattern in the NAYEON entry —
*"tends to play it safe and doesn't push dynamic range unless featured."* A
self-chosen minute is the ceiling of what someone does on favourable material,
not their level. And it inflates unevenly:

| | Round 0 reliability |
| --- | --- |
| **Performance** | reliable — presence shows on any material |
| **Musicality & Timing** | reliable — you can hear whether they're reading the track |
| **Technicality** | **optimistic** — easy choreography hides stiffness and short extension |
| **Execution** | **optimistic** — nothing is clean like something you already own |

Those two optimistic rows are exactly what a stretch piece exposes. So:

| before → after | old rule said | what was actually true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 → 5.5 | **cut** | flattering cover, real improvement |
| 3 → 5 | advance | honest cover, real improvement |
| 3 → 4 | cut | honest cover, little movement |
| **7 → 6.5** | **cut** | flattering cover, now at the floor |
| 8 → 8 | advance | above the bar, held steady |

Rows one and four are people improving and being cut for it. **And a discount
can't fix it** — the inflation isn't a fixed offset, it varies with how
favourable the chosen piece was, so no constant corrects it. Knocking 1.25 off
doesn't save 7 → 5.5.

Nor did the escape hatch help. *"Already ≥ 7.5 and didn't regress"* needs the
before to clear the bar, and the target is by definition their weakest thing, so
it almost never did. **The danger zone was a flattering baseline just below the
bar — the common case.**

The fix is structural rather than a correction factor: the before-number is now
a direct read of the one named thing, made with the assigned piece in front of
you, by the same people who will score the after. Still a judgment. But a
judgment of the same question, in the same units, and taken before the thing it
will be compared against exists.

**Round 0 keeps its real job**, which was never the numbers: a yes or a no on
whether someone belongs in the process, and a written document they can work
from either way. For a returning Diamond that document is the whole round — its
*"at most 3 opportunities to grow"* are where to go looking when you come to
pick their Round 1 piece, which is the same vocabulary with no second exercise.

## The three documents this generates

### Practice-cohort short form
Round 2's four scores, where someone got that far:
```
Technicality         6.25   whole body engaged, but isolations are soft
Execution            7.5    hits the pictures clean; held up in the hard section
Performance          5      present on your part, drops when you're not centered
Musicality & Timing  3.75   reads as muscle memory — the accents are on the count
```

### Round 0's document — both cohorts, and it carries the verdict
At least three strengths, each with the moment that showed it; at most three
opportunities to grow. **No scores**, because Round 0 doesn't produce any. The
pass or the fail goes out inside this same message, underneath the feedback
rather than above it — the reasons are the part worth something either way, and
a verdict at the top turns them into either a formality or a consolation prize.

Its growth areas are where to look when you pick that person's Round 1 piece.
They aren't the target — you write the target yourself at assignment, as one
specific thing — but they're the shortlist.

### The result
Round 1: the one named thing, both ends of it, and what moved. Round 2: the four
scores, weighted, against the bar and the floor. Each in the same vocabulary as
the document they got in Round 0, with the directors' own notes appended under
their own heading rather than woven into the verdict.

## Moving the bar

The bar is meant to move, and the plumbing assumes it. Two things make that
safe rather than messy.

**Round 2's outcome is derived, not stored.** Change the bar and every result
that hasn't gone out yet re-derives together, so a cohort is never split across
two standards — which is the thing that actually goes wrong when a number gets
adjusted mid-round. It's the same reasoning rank 12 gives for deriving
`auditionState`: a stored status that disagrees with the data is a bug you only
find by reading a card that says the wrong thing.

**A sent result freezes.** Once someone has been told, the stored outcome wins
whatever the bar does afterwards, because you can't unsend it. So the window for
moving the bar is "any time before results go out", and it's a config edit —
no deploy, nothing announced.

**It's already quiet from the outside.** The announcement never names a number;
it says *"if you meet the standard we're looking for, you're in."* So adjusting
7.5 between cycles, or before this cycle's results, is invisible to everyone
except you. Every score does record the standard it was judged against
(`scoredAgainst`) — internal only, and it's there so that *you* can answer "what
did this cohort actually clear?" a year from now, which is a question you'll want
answered rather than one you'll want hidden.

The only version I'd still avoid is moving it **after** some results have gone
out and others haven't, since that's the one case where two people in the same
round get judged differently and the records will show it.

If 7.5 turns out to be unreachable this cycle, the option I'd reach for first is
a second named outcome — *didn't clear the bar, but we want to work with you
toward next cycle* — rather than a lower number. It keeps the bar meaning one
thing while still moving people forward in a thin year.

## Still arguable

- **The floor of 6** — too strict, or not strict enough
- **The weights** — 40/30/20/10 puts musicality last, which is a strong claim
  about a dance unit; it also means the Round 1 target (the *lowest* dimension)
  can be the thing that counts least toward passing
- **What happens if nobody clears the bar** — see above
- **+1.25 as the Round 1 step** — one anchor is the natural unit, but two is a
  real improvement and one is within noise if you aren't calibrated
- **Whether they're told their number** or only their target dimension
